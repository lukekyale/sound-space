SYNG CELL ALPHA

2021

Wallpaper Magazine

Wallpaper: How long has it taken you to develop and launch Syng?

Christopher Stringer: Syng was founded three years before the launch of Cell Alpha. Before starting the company, Stringer spent time thinking about what he wanted to pursue after his career at Apple. His attention returned to audio, an interest that began when he was building speakers at fourteen years old.

Rather than looking at existing products for answers, he approached the speaker from a blank slate, asking what he personally wanted a speaker to become. This eventually developed into the idea of the Cell and the foundation of Syng.

W*: What is the primary idea behind the Cell Alpha?

CS: Cell Alpha was designed as more than an individual speaker. Syng wanted to create an architecture and platform capable of supporting new forms of sound and creative experiences over time.

The company organizes its approach around three disciplines: Design, Technology, and Culture. Rather than treating technology as separate from culture, Syng sees the product as something that can participate within the creative communities surrounding music and sound.

W*: What were the key inspirations behind the product ethos, as well as the design?

CS: The founding principle of Syng was to create a single device that could work alone or cooperate with multiple Cells in different arrangements, forming a unified sound field.

Stringer describes his design process as asking not what shape he wants to impose on a speaker, but what shape the speaker wants to be. Instead of beginning with appearance, the form develops from the purpose of the object: shaping sound within space.

W*: How did Triphonic sound come about?

CS: Triphonic combines hardware and software to adapt sound to the physical characteristics of a room. The system understands the position of each Cell and uses that information to map sound throughout the space.

Instead of organizing listening around traditional front, back, left, and right channels, Triphonic places the listener at the center. Sounds can be positioned, moved, enlarged, reduced, and layered throughout the environment.

W*: The Cell Alpha has a very industrial, robust look. How tailored is the design - is every component bespoke?

CS: Cell Alpha is built around eight custom drivers designed to operate together as one system. Its enclosure uses a new speaker architecture centered around the Triphone, a three-horned, 3D-printed sound projector.

The complexity of the speaker is concentrated into this single component, allowing the surrounding parts to remain comparatively simple. The resulting object uses reflection and transparency to interact with its environment.

W*: What is your opinion of smart speakers?

CS: Stringer sees smart speakers primarily as interfaces for voice assistants rather than devices designed around the fullest possibilities of sound. His interest instead lies in bringing high-fidelity distributed audio throughout the home.

Rather than designing in response to competing speaker companies, he argues that designers should identify their own purpose. His frustration with the existing speaker market became part of the motivation for imagining a different whole-home audio system.

W*: Do you envision creating more products in the future?

CS: Cell Alpha was intended to establish the visual and technological identity of Syng before developing a larger family of products. Future Cells would continue the recognizable Syng identity while expanding the company's approach to spatial audio.

Stringer also sees significant possibilities in software and Triphonic technology. Rather than defining every future use, Syng wants to create tools that allow artists and creators to discover new forms of immersive audio.
